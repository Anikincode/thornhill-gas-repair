Enbridge Red Tag Repair in Thornhill

Enbridge left a tag on your furnace, boiler or water heater? Here's what it says and how it gets cleared.

An Enbridge red tag is a Safety Violation notice. An "A" violation means the appliance is an immediate hazard and the gas is shut off right away. A "B" violation gives you up to 42 days to repair or replace it. Either way, a licensed contractor does the work and reports it to Enbridge.

This page covers A versus B, who can fix a tag, what happens if you wait and how we clear it.

What's the difference between an A and a B violation?

"A" violation"B" violation
What it meansAn immediate hazardNo immediate hazard
Your gasShut off right awayShut off if it isn't repaired in time
Your deadlineNo gas until it's fixedUp to 42 days to repair or replace

Read the notice to see which one you have, then call and tell us what it says.

Who can clear a red tag?

Not Enbridge. It says it "does not repair or service natural gas appliances," and tells customers to hire a TSSA-registered contractor.

That's us: we're licensed and insured.

Fill out the form, or call and we'll book it with you.

What happens if I wait?

Your gas gets shut off. Enbridge says: "If the hazard is not repaired the natural gas supply will be shut off." On a B violation Enbridge also has to verify the repair, and it notes "A service charge may be applied."

Forty-two days goes fast if a part has to be ordered.

How do you clear it?

  1. You call with the notice in hand.
  2. We diagnose the cause. It might be the appliance, the venting or the piping.
  3. We quote the fix. Then we do the work.
  4. We report the repair to Enbridge so your service stays on or comes back on.

Why do older Thornhill homes get red tags?

The tag gives a deadline. Don't let it run out.

  • Original equipment. A furnace or water heater from when the house was built can fail a safety check on venting or combustion.

  • Rental water heaters. If your water heater is rented, check the rental agreement first. The rental company may be the one that has to repair or replace it.

  • Blocked or corroded vents. Older flue pipes in finished basements and utility rooms are a common cause.

  • Earlier DIY work. A previous owner's appliance hookup is one of the things an Enbridge visit can flag.

Is a rented water heater my problem or the rental company's?

If the tank is rented, the rental company owns it, and a venting or installation fault on its unit is worth raising with them before you pay for a repair. Read your rental agreement, and send them a photo of the tag. We'll tell you what the tag says and what's wrong either way.

What if I don't agree with the tag?

Ask the person who wrote it, or the contractor looking at it, to point to the exact item. Codes can be read differently, and you're free to get a second opinion from another licensed contractor. Until the item is fixed or cleared, the deadline on the notice still runs, so don't wait.
