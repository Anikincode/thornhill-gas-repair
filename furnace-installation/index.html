High-efficiency or standard?

High-efficiency furnaces get more heat from the same gas. They vent through plastic pipe instead of the chimney, take combustion air from outside and need a condensate drain. Which one fits depends on your home's venting and how long you'll stay.

How is a furnace sized?

By a heat-loss calculation for the house, not by the size of the old furnace. A furnace that's one or two sizes too big short-cycles and heats unevenly. Two furnaces with the same output can also differ in efficiency, so compare both.

What else may change?

A replacement can also mean new venting, a condensate drain or pump, ductwork changes, or a gas line or electrical change. A like-for-like swap is simpler. Moving the vent or changing the setup adds steps, and we'll tell you before we start which one yours is.

What should I ask an installer?

  • Will you do a heat-loss calculation, and can I see it?
  • What's included in the quote?
  • Who does the gas connection and the pressure test?
  • Who takes care of the permits?

How does an install go?

  1. Shut off and remove the old furnace.
  2. Set the new furnace and connect the ductwork.
  3. Run gas and venting and pressure test the gas connection.
  4. Commission it and set the thermostat.

LOWER YOUR ENERGY USE

Why a newer furnace can cost less to run

A high-efficiency furnace pulls more heat out of each cubic metre of gas and sends less of it up the vent. It also runs in steps instead of full blast, which keeps rooms steadier.

Whether it pays off for you depends on your current furnace, your home and how long you plan to stay. We'll give you the repair number and the replacement number side by side and let you decide.
