Gas water heater repair finds why a tank isn't heating or is leaking, then fixes the failed part, such as the thermostat, the burner, the pilot assembly or the venting. A leaking tank usually can't be repaired and needs replacing. In Thornhill the gas side of the work belongs to a licensed contractor.

This page covers common problems, why a pilot won't stay lit, what's fixable and what isn't, and how the repair goes.

What are common gas water heater problems?

  • No hot water, or not enough

  • The pilot won't stay lit

  • Rumbling, popping or banging from the tank

  • Water around the base of the tank

  • A burning smell or a red tag

Why won't the pilot stay lit?

The usual cause is the thermocouple, the safety sensor beside the pilot flame. Other causes are a draft that blows the pilot out, low gas pressure, a clogged pilot line and poor venting. Relighting it again and again doesn't fix any of these, and it's not something to keep trying.

Why is my water heater rumbling?

Minerals and sediment collect at the bottom of the tank, and the water trapped under them boils and pops. A flush often fixes the noise if the tank is still sound. If it's old and noisy, tell us when you call.

Is it the pilot, the thermostat or the tank?

  • Cold water only often means the pilot has gone out.

  • Not enough hot water can mean a thermostat set too low, a failed part, or a tank that's too small for the household.

  • Hot water that runs out fast can also mean sediment in the tank.

Can a leaking tank be repaired?

Usually not. Water coming from the tank body means the tank has failed. A leak at a fitting or a valve is a different story, and often fixable. We'll check which it is.

How does a repair go?

  1. We confirm the gas supply and the venting are safe.
  2. We find the failed part.
  3. We quote the repair before starting.
  4. We fix it and test the burner and the gas connection.

What's different about water heaters in Thornhill homes?

  • Large households. Houses with several bathrooms ask more of one tank, and a failing element or burner shows up fast.

  • Rented tanks. Many Ontario homes rent their water heater, so check who owns it before we repair it.

  • Older utility rooms. Tanks installed in the 1990s and 2000s are reaching the end of their life.
