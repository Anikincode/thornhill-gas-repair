Gas Leak Repair in Thornhill

Gas leak repair starts after the leak has been made safe. If you smell gas in Thornhill, leave the building and call Enbridge at 1-866-763-5427 or 911 from outside. Once Enbridge has cleared it, a licensed contractor finds the source, repairs it and pressure tests the line.

This page covers what to do first, how to spot a leak, how we find the source and what a finished repair looks like.

Smell gas? Do this first

These are Enbridge's steps:

  1. Get out. Leave doors and windows open behind you if you can do that without delay.
  2. Don't touch anything electrical. No switches, no phones inside, no smoking, and don't start a vehicle nearby.
  3. Call from a safe place. Enbridge's 24-hour line is 1-866-763-5427. Or call 911.

Don't hunt for the leak and don't try to fix it. If gas is burning, call 911.

How can I tell it's a gas leak?

  • A rotten egg smell. Natural gas has no smell of its own, so a smell is added on purpose.
  • A hissing sound near a pipe, a meter or an appliance
  • A flame that burns yellow or orange instead of blue
  • Dead or dying plants around a buried line

A carbon monoxide alarm won't warn you here. Enbridge says CO alarms detect carbon monoxide, "not natural gas leaks."

How do you find and fix the leak?

  1. We isolate the section so no gas reaches the problem area.
  2. We locate the source. It might be a fitting, a valve, a flex connector, a corroded pipe or a damaged line.
  3. We replace the failed part, not just tighten it.
  4. We pressure test the line. A leak repair isn't finished until the test holds.
  5. We relight and check your appliances.

Why does a leak keep coming back?

Usually because the part that failed was patched, not replaced, or because the line has a second weak joint nobody checked. That's why we test the whole run, not only the spot that smelled.

What does a leak repair cost?

It depends on what failed and how hard it is to reach. We quote it after we've found the source, and before we do the work.

What makes a gas leak more likely in Thornhill homes?

  • Houses from the 1960s to the 1990s. Much of Thornhill was built in those decades, so original steel pipe and fittings are now decades old.
  • Many gas appliances per house. A range, dryer, fireplace, furnace, water heater, barbecue and sometimes a pool heater means many joints that can weep.
  • Renovations. Finished basements, additions and kitchen remodels move or strain gas pipe.
  • Winter. Heavy snow and ice on a meter or vent can cause trouble, and Enbridge asks owners to keep both clear.

Whichever applies to you, the first step is the same: leave the building and call Enbridge.

What if the leak is on an outdoor or pool heater line?

Pool heater, barbecue and fire-feature lines are common leak points because they run outdoors, through the ground and often through a fence or deck. If Enbridge has tagged one, shut the appliance off, don't use it, and call us. The line gets pressure tested after the repair.
